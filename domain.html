Academic Foundation

1. Literature Survey

Globally, at least 2.2 billion people live with near or distance vision impairments (World Health Organization, 2026). While conventional assistive systems combine optical character recognition (OCR) and text-to-speech (TTS), existing literature reveals that reading printed materials independently is a multi-stage challenge spanning physical positioning, document comprehension, layout deconstruction, and cognitive personalization.

Module 01 Domain

Wearable Camera Guidance & Edge Vision

Wearable reading research began with finger-mounted scanning devices such as FingerReader (Shilkrot et al., MIT Media Lab [2]) and Finger-eye (Liu et al. [4]), which demonstrated tactile reading but lacked automated full-page framing. Later embedded systems including Vivoice (Prabha et al. [5]), Viet et al. [4], and wearable ring readers (Dubey et al. [6]) demonstrated Raspberry Pi camera capture. However, they assume clear static images are already available. VisionMate addresses this pre-capture gap by deploying real-time YOLO11n object detection and OpenCV boundary/margin algorithms directly on Raspberry Pi 5 to provide audible directional guidance.

Module 02 Domain

Document Classification & Vision-Language Models

Document image classification research (Liu et al. [1]; Appalaraju et al., DocFormer [3]) demonstrates that identifying document genre is essential for tailoring reading workflows. For wearable devices, lightweight inverted-residual networks such as MobileNetV2 (Sandler et al. [2]) provide high accuracy with low computational footprint. Furthermore, assistive accessibility studies like VizWiz (Gurari et al. [6], [7]) prove that blind users require descriptions of non-textual imagery. VisionMate leverages Salesforce BLIP (Li et al. [4]) and Microsoft Florence-2 (Xiao et al. [5]) with accessibility post-filtering to describe book covers, illustrations, charts, and diagrams that OCR engines ignore.

Module 03 Domain

Document Layout Analysis & Local Summarization

Complex printed materials such as newspapers and magazines present multi-column body text, section headers, and dispersed articles. Classical layout analysis (Mitchell & Yan [5]; Palfray et al. [6]) and recent spatial benchmarks (DocBed [7]; PubLayNet [17]; DocBank [18]) confirm that raw sequential OCR interleaves neighbouring columns. Broken News (Agarwal et al. [8]) demonstrated the necessity of structural reconstruction for print-impaired readers. VisionMate couples local Tesseract OCR with DocLayout-YOLO (Zhao et al. [21]) and graph-based article grouping $G=(V,E)$, categorizing stories into 7 semantic classes via TF-IDF Logistic Regression, offering hands-free Vosk navigation, and summarizing content with local grounded Llama 3.2:3B (Ollama).

Module 04 Domain

Personalized Reading & Session Continuity

Personalized text simplification literature (Bingel [14]; ACCESS, Martin et al. [7]) demonstrates that text difficulty is user-dependent, meaning fixed-vocabulary TTS fails diverse readers. Sequence-to-sequence transformers like T5 (Raffel et al. [8]; Alva-Manchego et al. [9]) enable controllable multi-level simplification. In speech processing, acoustic Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC, Davis & Mermelstein [11]) combined with Support Vector Machines (SVM, Cortes & Vapnik [12]; Wang & Hu [10]) provide robust voice-tone classification. VisionMate integrates persistent contactless RFID identification (Want [13]) with a local SQLite preference database and deterministic speech-completion synchronization to create a continuous closed reading loop.

Research Problem

"Printed Accessibility Demands Multi-Stage Document Understanding, Not Raw OCR-to-Speech."

Traditional assistive reading technologies model reading as a simplistic, one-shot pipeline: Image → OCR → Text → Speech. This conventional approach breaks down in real-world scenarios because printed media is visually, structurally, and linguistically complex.

The Four Core Accessibility Failures: 1. Pre-Capture Positioning Failure: Blind users cannot verify if a page is in frame, at the right focal distance, or tilted.
2. Visual Content Blindness: Standard OCR reads only text characters, discarding cover artwork, photographs, and charts.
3. Structural Layout Scrambling: Multi-column newspapers interleave separate stories into disjointed audio fragments.
4. Linguistic & Session Inflexibility: Rigid vocabulary overwhelms users, and absence of persistent profiles requires repetitive manual setup.
Proposed Solution

VisionMate: An Autonomous, Edge-Assisted Wearable Framework

VisionMate transforms assistive reading into an intelligent, user-directed closed loop that spans wearable sensing, deep vision-language understanding, graph-based article reconstruction, and profile-driven personalized speech delivery.

The Closed-Loop VisionMate Sequence: User taps RFID card → Device retrieves stored preferences → Camera guidance gives directional cues → Auto-capture takes high-res frame → Document type is classified → Visual graphics described aloud → Layout analyzed & articles reconstructed → User selects topic via Vosk voice commands → Llama 3.2:3B generates grounded summary → T5 simplifies vocabulary → Audio delivered via I2S stereo speakers → Audible page-turn prompt re-engages camera guidance for next page.
Comprehensive System Workflow

The Five Coordinated Stages of VisionMate

Derived from the unified system architecture (Figure 1) presented across all four engineering research theses.

Stage 1: Identity & Profile (M4)

RFID Tap → SQLite Profile

MFRC522 contactless card resolves user ID, loading reading level, voice gender, pace, and tone with a bounded 3-attempt spoken retry for returning users.

Stage 2: Guidance & Capture (M1)

YOLO11n + OpenCV → Auto-Capture

Raspberry Pi 5 and UGREEN 4K camera detect page borders (99.4% mAP50), issuing voice guidance ("Move closer/left") and triggering auto-capture upon stable distance/visibility.

Stage 3: Document & Visual AI (M2)

MobileNetV2 + BLIP & Florence-2

Classifies 5 document genres (87.27% accuracy), extracts titles via OCR, and generates natural-language captions for cover art (BLIP) and inside diagrams (Florence-2).

Stage 4: Layout & Summary (M3)

DocLayout-YOLO → Vosk → Llama 3.2

Reconstructs multi-column articles via graph logic $G=(V,E)$, categorizes into 7 classes (85.71% accuracy), accepts Vosk speech choices, and generates grounded local summaries.

Stage 5: Adaptivity & Continuity Loop (M4 & M1)

T5 Simplification → PCA-SVM Audio → Deterministic Page Turn

T5-small adapts language difficulty across 3 tiers (BLEU 62.85). Speech is styled via PCA-SVM (82.72%) and delivered through dual MAX98357 I2S amplifiers. Playback completion triggers an audible prompt ("Reading complete. Please turn to the next page."), smoothly re-engaging camera guidance for subsequent pages without restarting session configuration.

Project Goals

4. Research Objectives & Component Breakdown

VisionMate is driven by an overarching project mission realized through four dedicated, experimentally evaluated sub-objectives across our final-year cohort.

Overarching Project Aim

"To design, develop, and evaluate VisionMate: an autonomous, edge-assisted smart wearable reading assistant that empowers visually impaired individuals to independently access and read printed materials through real-time voice-guided camera positioning and auto-capture, automated document-type identification and multimodal image description, layout-aware article reconstruction with semantic categorization and local summarization, and profile-driven personalized reading with deterministic page-completion control in an end-to-end closed reading loop."

01

Objective 1: Camera Position Guidance & Auto-Capture

Lead: Priyashantha D G M D · IT22295392

Develop a real-time computer vision module utilizing a trained YOLO11n page detector (99.4% mAP50 on 309 images) and OpenCV boundary refinement running locally on Raspberry Pi 5. Calculate calibrated area-ratio distance and 4-edge margin visibility, provide spoken spatial guidance cues, and execute rapid auto-capture without requiring strict centring. Integrate the complete physical prototype including UGREEN 4K camera, dual MAX98357 I2S 3W amplifiers, stereo speakers, power bank, and USB-C power circuitry.

02

Objective 2: Document Identification & Image Describing

Lead: D C M A Chithrasena · IT22114112

Train a 5-class MobileNetV2 transfer-learning model on 1,095 images to classify Magazine, Newspaper, Novel, Printed Letter, and Report (87.27% test accuracy, 90.91% validation). Implement document-specific title/masthead extraction via EasyOCR and PaddleOCR fallback. Deploy local vision-language models—Salesforce BLIP for book/magazine cover captioning and Microsoft Florence-2 for inside diagrams, illustrations, and maps—with accessibility post-filtering via dedicated FastAPI endpoints.

03

Objective 3: Content Summarization & Category Detection

Lead: Herath H M M G E H B · IT22198136

Implement hybrid document layout analysis fusing local Tesseract OCR with DocLayout-YOLO (DocStructBench 1024) and OpenCV geometry to infer reading lanes and reconstruct multi-column newspaper articles using a graph-based representation $G=(V,E)$ supporting partial captures. Classify articles into 7 semantic categories via TF-IDF + Logistic Regression (85.71% test accuracy on 6,764 dataset), provide offline Vosk voice selection, and generate grounded local summaries via Llama 3.2:3B on Ollama.

04

Objective 4: Personalized Reading & Page Completion

Lead: Praveesha R G R · IT22299970

Implement persistent contactless RFID user identification linked to a local SQLite database storing reading level, preferred voice, pace, and tone with a bounded 3-attempt spoken recovery. Fine-tune a sequence-to-sequence T5-small model for 3-tier text simplification (Very Simple, Moderate, Light; BLEU 62.85, 83% accuracy on 1,796 records). Classify voice styles using a 135-feature PCA-SVM model (82.72% accuracy), and synchronize deterministic speech-end page completion to cue page turns and close the reading loop.

System Architecture

5. High-Level Methodology & Dataflow

The end-to-end processing pipeline orchestrates physical sensing, edge vision processing, multimodal AI inferences, and personalized acoustic synthesis.

Physical System Embodiment

Hardware Integration & Wearable Architecture

The complete VisionMate software suite executes on a standalone wearable headset prototype documented in research component 1. The hardware architecture integrates a Raspberry Pi 5 processing unit, a UGREEN 4K USB webcam for video frame acquisition, an MFRC522 contactless RFID reader, dual MAX98357 I2S 3W Class-D amplifier breakout DAC modules powering stereo speakers, and a portable power bank regulated via a dedicated power switch and USB Type-C power path.

Raspberry Pi 5 (8GB) UGREEN 4K Webcam Dual MAX98357 I2S DACs Stereo Speakers MFRC522 RFID USB-C Power Circuit

Modular Technical Breakdown Across All Four Subsystems

Subsystem 1 Lead: Priyashantha D G M D

Automatic Camera Position Guidance & Auto-Capture

Continuous video feed from the UGREEN 4K camera is processed by a trained YOLO11n model to detect visible page regions (0.994 mAP50 on 309 images). OpenCV contour processing refines true page borders with fallback to the YOLO bounding box. Distance is estimated using calibrated page area ratios, and full visibility is verified via 4-edge margins. Auditory guidance ("Move closer", "Move camera left", "Ready. Hold steady") directs the user, triggering fast auto-capture (READY_STABLE_FRAMES = 1, 8s cooldown) without requiring strict centring.

YOLO11n (0.994 mAP50) OpenCV Boundary Refinement Calibrated Area Ratio Fast Auto-Capture Dual I2S MAX98357 Audio
Subsystem 2 Lead: D C M A Chithrasena

Document Identification and Image Describing

Classifies 5 document types (Magazine, Newspaper, Novel, Printed Letter, Report) using a transfer-learning MobileNetV2 model trained on a balanced 1,095-image dataset (87.27% test accuracy, 90.91% validation). Extracts cover titles via EasyOCR/PaddleOCR and articulates natural-language visual descriptions using Salesforce BLIP (covers) and Microsoft Florence-2 (inside figures and illustrations) via FastAPI endpoints.

Subsystem 3 Lead: Herath H M M G E H B

Content Summarization & Category Detection

Deconstructs complex multi-column newspaper pages using Tesseract OCR, OpenCV, and DocLayout-YOLO. Infers adaptive reading lanes and reconstructs articles via graph-based algorithms $G=(V, E)$. Classifies articles across 7 categories using TF-IDF + Logistic Regression (85.71% accuracy on 6,764 dataset). Employs offline Vosk voice commands and generates concise summaries with Llama 3.2:3B via Ollama with grounding checks.
